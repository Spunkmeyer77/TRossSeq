1What TRoss Seq does

TRoss Seq is a 16-step drum sequencer that plays your drum boxes over MIDI, locked to your DAW's tempo: hardware drum boxes, and software drum boxes, whether they're plug-ins in your DAW or apps running on their own.

  • 13 drum lanes plus an accent row, 16 steps each, programmed on lit step pads.
  • Every lane sends its own MIDI note on its own channel, so one TRoss Seq can play one drum box or several at once, hardware or software.
  • 12 pattern slots. A new slot always waits for the current pattern to finish, so changes land on the bar. Chain them (up to 32 steps), locked to the song position.
  • A drum-aware randomiser that writes patterns the way each drum is usually played, with styles, density, lane locks, variations, fills and undo.
  • Two ways out: straight to a MIDI port on your computer, or to your DAW's MIDI routing.
  • Flams, drags and ruffs on any step (Shift-click, right-click to edit), and a drum map library to keep one map per drum box, with built-in maps for hardware and software drum boxes.
  • Swing, gate, accent velocity, MIDI clock out, MIDI learn, keyboard recording and MIDI file export.

TRoss Seq makes no sound of its own. It is an instrument plug-in that outputs MIDI, which is why it loads on a MIDI or instrument track.

2Installing

macOS

  1. Open TRossSeq-0.9.0-macOS.pkg (the manual is in the same download).
  2. The installer isn't signed, so the first time macOS blocks it. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway, then open the .pkg again.
  3. Follow the installer. It puts the Audio Unit in /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components and the VST3 in /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3. Click Customize to install only one of them.
  4. In Ableton Live, open Settings → Plug-Ins, check that Audio Units (and/or VST3) are switched on, then hold Option and click Rescan.

Logic and other Audio Unit hosts find it under AU Instruments → Ticco Ross; Live and Bitwig under VST3 → Ticco Ross. Section 3 has the setup for each.

Windows

Run TRossSeq-0.9.0-Windows-Setup.exe. If SmartScreen appears, click More info → Run anyway. The VST3 goes to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3. Rescan plug-ins in your DAW.

Linux

Unpack the .tar.gz and run ./install.sh. It copies the VST3 to ~/.vst3 and the LV2 to ~/.lv2, with no root needed. ./uninstall.sh removes them.

Updating

Install the new version over the old one, then rescan. Your projects keep their patterns and settings. The version number is shown at the top right of the panel.

3Setting up in Live, Logic and Bitwig

Pick the route for the kind of drum box you're playing, then follow the steps for your DAW.

Drum boxRouteWhy
HardwareMIDI OUT PORT → your MIDI interface or the drum box's USB MIDIWorks the same in every DAW, keeps every lane on its own channel, and has its own tight timing.
Software plug-inHOST OUT → routed by the DAW to the plug-in's trackStays inside the DAW, nothing extra to set up.
Software app (standalone)MIDI OUT PORT → the app's MIDI input, or a virtual MIDI portThe app runs outside the DAW, so it needs a MIDI port (section 6).

In every DAW: open PRESETS and pick the map for your drum box (or set each lane's NOTE and CH, section 6), then press play in the DAW. TRoss Seq follows its tempo, start, stop and loops. Use one route per drum box: with both MIDI OUT PORT and HOST OUT reaching the same box, every note plays twice.

Ableton Live

Use the VST3 version: Plug-Ins → VST3 → Ticco Ross → TRoss Seq. Live can't take MIDI out of AU plug-ins, so the AU version only works with MIDI OUT PORT.

Hardware drum box

  1. Create a MIDI track and drop TRoss Seq on it.
  2. Under MIDI OUT PORT, pick your interface or drum box. The line underneath should read CONNECTED.

Software drum box (plug-in)

  1. Put TRoss Seq (VST3) on a MIDI track. Set MIDI OUT PORT to NONE and leave HOST OUT on.
  2. Put the drum plug-in on a second MIDI track.
  3. On that second track, set MIDI From to the TRoss Seq track, set the chooser below it to TRoss Seq, and set Monitor to In.

Live merges all MIDI channels into one when it routes between tracks. That's fine for one drum plug-in; to play several plug-ins on different channels, use one TRoss Seq per plug-in.

To play or record from your keyboard, arm the TRoss Seq track (or set its monitor to In).

Logic Pro

Logic uses the Audio Unit. On a Software Instrument track, click the Instrument slot → AU Instruments → Ticco Ross → TRoss Seq. If it doesn't appear, open Logic Pro → Settings → Plug-in Manager and rescan it.

Hardware drum box: under MIDI OUT PORT, pick your interface or drum box.

Software drum box (plug-in), Logic 11 or later

  1. On the TRoss Seq track, set MIDI OUT PORT to NONE and leave HOST OUT on.
  2. Create a second Software Instrument track with the drum plug-in.
  3. In that track's Track Inspector, set Internal MIDI In to Instrument Output and pick the TRoss Seq track as the source. Set MIDI In Channel to the lanes' channel (or All).
  4. Turn on Input Monitoring on that track and press play.

Logic 10 and earlier can't route MIDI out of an instrument plug-in. There, send TRoss Seq to a virtual MIDI port (section 6), keep the drum plug-in's track selected, and switch TRoss Seq's MIDI THRU off so the notes don't loop back into it.

Bitwig Studio

Bitwig uses the VST3 (also on Linux). Drag TRoss Seq from the browser's plug-ins (Ticco Ross) onto an Instrument track.

Hardware drum box: under MIDI OUT PORT, pick your interface or drum box.

Software drum box (plug-in)

  1. On the TRoss Seq track, set MIDI OUT PORT to NONE and leave HOST OUT on.
  2. Create a second Instrument track with the drum plug-in.
  3. In that track's input (the I/O section), pick the TRoss Seq track as its note source, and set the accepted channel to the lanes' channel (or all). Or add a Note Receiver device before the plug-in with the TRoss Seq track as its source.
  4. If you hear nothing, arm the track or switch its monitoring on.

5Getting the notes to your drum machine

There are two outputs. Use one or both, but don't send both to the same drum box, or every note plays twice.

OutputHow it worksUse it in
MIDI OUT PORTTRoss Seq sends straight to the port you pick: a MIDI interface, or a drum box with USB MIDI. It has its own high-priority timing, so notes don't jitter with the DAW's buffer.Every DAW. The way to go for hardware drum boxes.
HOST OUTThe notes go to the DAW's MIDI output for the plug-in, and you route them from there.Software drum box plug-ins: Live (VST3 version), Logic 11 or later, Bitwig, Reaper, Cubase, Studio One… (section 3).

MIDI OUT PORT

Click the selector to see the ports connected right now; the list refreshes each time it opens. NONE switches the port output off. The status line below tells you what's going on:

StatusMeaning
CONNECTEDNotes are going to the port.
NOT CONNECTED…The project asks for a port that isn't plugged in. Plug it in and pick it again, or pick another port.
COULDN'T OPEN…Another program holds the port (common on Windows; see section 16).

PORT DELAY

Lines the drum box up with the audio coming out of your DAW, from −250 to +250 ms. The knob sits at 0 (no shift); double-click it to go back there.

  • Positive (turn right): everything sent to the port goes out later. Use it when the drum box plays ahead of the DAW's audio, which is the usual case, because the DAW's own sound is delayed by its audio buffer. Start at about your buffer size (in ms).
  • Negative (turn left): everything sent to the port goes out earlier. Use it when the drum box is late, for example a slow USB or MIDI interface, a drum box that reacts slowly, or a software drum box whose audio comes back through a long chain. TRoss Seq runs that much ahead of the song for the port, so the hits still land on the beat.

Adjust by ear against a sound in the DAW. PORT DELAY only moves the MIDI OUT PORT (notes and MIDI clock). HOST OUT always stays exactly on the grid, and MIDI THRU and clicks on the name strips are never moved.

With a negative delay, the very first step after pressing play can't be sent before you pressed play, so it goes out right away. Over loops, every step is still sent once, and only once.

CLOCK OUT

Sends MIDI clock (24 ticks per quarter note), Start, Stop, Continue and Song Position to the port. Switch it on if your drum box has tempo-synced effects or its own sequencer that should follow along, and set the drum box to sync to external / MIDI clock. Clock goes to the port only.

MIDI THRU

Passes the MIDI coming into the track (your keyboard or pads) through to the outputs, so you can play the drum box live through TRoss Seq. In Live, arm the track (or set its monitor to In) so your keyboard reaches the plug-in.

6Tracks: names, notes, channels

Each of the 13 lanes has a name strip on the left and its MIDI settings on the right.

RenameShift-click the name and type up to 10 characters; Return keeps it, Esc cancels. The name also tells the randomiser what kind of drum the lane is (section 12).

CHThe MIDI channel, 1–16. Drag up or down.

Check your drum box. The preset maps follow each maker's published default notes. If a sound doesn't trigger, look up its note in the drum box's manual or MIDI settings and fix that lane with LEARN or the NOTE read-out.

Playing a software drum box

Plug-ins running in your DAW: see section 3 for Live, Logic and Bitwig.

Standalone apps (a software drum box running on its own, outside the DAW): pick the app's MIDI input under MIDI OUT PORT if it creates one. If it doesn't, use a virtual MIDI port and set the app to listen to it. TRoss Seq still follows your DAW's tempo. To make a virtual port on a Mac:

  1. Open Audio MIDI Setup → Window → Show MIDI Studio, double-click IAC Driver and tick Device is online.
  2. In TRoss Seq, set MIDI OUT PORT to IAC Driver Bus 1 and pick the software's map in PRESETS.
  3. In the app, choose IAC Driver Bus 1 as its MIDI input.
  4. Make sure the app listens on the channel the lanes send on (CH), or on all channels.

On Windows, use a virtual MIDI port such as loopMIDI the same way. If your DAW also listens to that virtual port, switch it off as an input there, so the notes don't loop back into TRoss Seq.

Using several drum boxes

Give each drum box its own MIDI channel and set the lanes' CH to match: for example lanes 1–8 on channel 10 for one box, lanes 9–13 on channel 11 for another. Both boxes can sit on the same MIDI port (daisy-chained through MIDI THRU) or you can use two TRoss Seq instances with a port each.

7Mute and solo

ActionResult
Click MMutes the lane (the button lights yellow). Click again to unmute.
Click SSolo. The button lights blue. As soon as any S is lit, only the soloed lanes play, even if they are muted; every other lane goes quiet. Light several S buttons to hear those lanes together. Click again to switch the solo off; when no S is lit, the mutes count again.
Shift + hold MMomentary solo. The button turns blue and shows S; only that lane plays, even if it is muted itself, and every other lane goes quiet. Let go of the mouse button and everything is back exactly as it was. Nothing is toggled. It beats the S buttons while you hold it.

Mutes and solos can be automated in your DAW (Track 1 Mute … Track 13 Mute, Track 1 Solo … Track 13 Solo) and are saved with the project. They don't affect clicks on the name strips or MIDI THRU.

8Programming patterns

  • Click a pad to switch a step on or off. Drag along a lane to paint several steps on (or off, if you started on a lit pad).
  • The pads are coloured in groups of four, so the beats are easy to see. The LEDs above follow the playing step, and the playing column gets a white outline.
  • ACCENT row: on any step where it's lit, every lane plays at the ACCENT velocity instead of the VELOCITY.

Flams, drags and ruffs

A flam is a quick, softer grace note just before the main hit, like a drummer's two sticks landing almost together. A drag has two grace notes and a ruff three.

  • Shift-click a pad to add a flam. If the step was off, it switches on with the flam. Shift-click again to remove it; the step stays on. A plain click switches the step off, flam included.
  • Grace notes play before the step, so the main hit stays exactly on the grid. The pad shows a small column of tiny LEDs on its left edge, one per grace note (1 flam, 2 drag, 3 ruff), and the main LED moves a little to the right. A thin line under the main LED means the step has its own spacing or level.
  • The FLAM knob sets how far before the step the grace notes start, and FLAM LVL how loud they are (section 11). Every flam follows these knobs unless you give it its own settings.

Right-click a pad to edit its flam:

Menu itemWhat it does
Step on / offSwitches the step.
No flam · Flam · Drag · RuffHow many grace notes: none, 1, 2 or 3. Picking one on an empty or plain step adds it.
SpacingFLAM knob, or a fixed 12, 25, 40 or 60 ms for this step. The grace notes of a drag or ruff are spread evenly over that time.
Grace levelFLAM LEVEL knob, or a fixed 30, 50, 75 or 100 % of the step's velocity for this step.
Use this flam on every hit in this laneCopies this step's flam type, spacing and level to every step of the lane in the playing pattern.
Remove all flams in this laneRemoves the lane's flams in the playing pattern; the steps stay.

Flams work on every lane except ACCENT, are saved with the project, can be undone with UNDO (whole-lane changes) and are included in EXPORT MIDI. Flams are only ever set by you: the randomiser never adds them (section 12). Removing a flam forgets its settings, so adding it again starts as a plain flam.

TRoss Seq always follows the DAW: it starts, stops, loops and jumps with the transport, and step 1 falls on the downbeat of each bar. When the transport stops, any note still held gets its note-off.

9Recording from a keyboard or pads

With REC on (it is by default) and the song playing, every incoming note that matches a lane is written into the nearest step of the playing pattern. Swing is taken into account.

  • A note matches a lane by note and channel first, then by note alone, so a keyboard on channel 1 still records into lanes that send on channel 10.
  • Velocity above 100 also sets the accent on that step.
  • Clicking a name strip while playing records too (Cmd / Ctrl-click for an accent).
  • While the transport is stopped, nothing is recorded; hits just play.

Switch REC off to play along without writing anything.

10The 12 pattern slots

  • Click a pattern button to select it. While the song plays, the new slot waits until the current pattern reaches step 1 (its LED blinks while queued), so changes always land cleanly. While stopped, it switches at once.
  • The LED shows the slot that's playing; the light cap shows the selected one. A small dash marks empty slots.
  • Right-click a pattern button to Copy to another slot, or Clear it.
  • The Pattern parameter can be automated, so you can arrange pattern changes in your DAW's timeline (not while a chain is set).

Chaining patterns

A chain plays several patterns one after another, for example a groove three times and a fill once.

  • Shift-click pattern buttons in the order you want them, up to 32 steps. Shift-clicking the same button again repeats it: 1, 1, 1, 2 plays pattern 1 three times, then pattern 2.
  • Each pattern plays its full LENGTH, then the next one starts on step 1. At the end, the chain starts again.
  • The chain is locked to the song position: bar 1 of the song plays chain step 1, bar 2 step 2, and so on (a "bar" being one pattern LENGTH). Jumping, looping or starting in the middle of the song always lands on the right pattern.
  • Chained buttons show a dim LED; the one playing is bright. The display shows CHN 2/4 P1: chain step 2 of 4, playing pattern 1.
  • Editing the pads changes the pattern that's playing in the chain.
  • A plain click on a pattern button ends the chain and goes back to that single pattern.
  • Right-click a pattern button for Add to the chain, Remove the last chain step and Clear the chain. UNDO takes back chain changes. The chain is saved with the project, and EXPORT MIDI can write it as one clip.

11Feel: velocity, accent, gate, swing, flam

KnobRangeWhat it does
VELOCITY1–127Velocity of normal steps. Default 100.
ACCENT1–127Velocity of steps with the ACCENT row lit, and of Cmd / Ctrl-clicked name strips. Default 127.
GATE5–95 %How long each note is held, as a fraction of a 16th note. Most drum boxes only care about the note-on; longer gates matter for sounds that respond to note length.
SWING50–75 %50 % is straight. Higher values push every second 16th (steps 2, 4, 6…) later: 54–58 % is a light shuffle, 66.7 % a triplet feel, 75 % the maximum.
FLAM5–80 msHow far before a flammed step its grace notes start. Default 25 ms; 10–20 ms is tight, 40 ms and up sounds like separate hits. Never more than half a step. Steps with their own spacing (right-click a pad) ignore it.
FLAM LVL10–100 %How loud grace notes are, as a percentage of the step's velocity. Default 60 %. Steps with their own grace level ignore it.

12The randomiser

The randomiser writes patterns lane by lane, the way each drum is usually played, instead of throwing random dots on the grid.

ControlWhat it does
RANDOMWrites a whole new pattern into the playing slot. Every unlocked lane is rewritten over the current LENGTH. The ACCENT row is never changed.
Shift-click RANDOMA variation: only a few steps of a few lanes change, and the anchors (kick on step 1, the backbeat) stay. Good for keeping a groove alive without losing it.
FILLRewrites only the last four steps of the pattern with a fill: toms, snare, hats and percussion. Press it before the bar you want the fill in.
UNDOGoes back to the pattern as it was before the last RANDOM, variation, fill, lane randomise, CLEAR, lane clear or whole-lane flam change; up to 32 steps back. Greyed out when there's nothing to undo.
DENSITYHow busy random patterns are. 50 % follows the style as designed; lower is sparser, higher is busier. The anchors (kick on 1, the backbeat in STRAIGHT) are kept at any density.
RANDOM STYLEThe character of what RANDOM and the variation write (below).
Alt-click a name
(Option-click on a Mac)		Locks the lane: a padlock appears and RANDOM, the variation and FILL leave it alone. Alt-click again to unlock. Locks are saved with the project.
Right-click a name → Randomise this laneRolls just that one lane, even if it's locked.

Timing

While the song plays, a random result waits for step 1, so the pattern never changes in the middle of a bar. The RANDOM key's light blinks and the display shows PAT 1 > RND until it takes over. Press RANDOM again while it's waiting to roll a different one; press UNDO to cancel it. While stopped, results are written at once.

Styles

StyleCharacter
STRAIGHTFour on the floor: kick on the beats with the odd extra, snare always on the backbeat (steps 5 and 13), 8th-note hats with some 16ths, open hats on the off-beats, toms near the end of the bar.

Flams and the randomiser

The randomiser never adds flams. RANDOM and Randomise this lane remove the flams on the lanes they rewrite; locked lanes keep theirs, and variations and FILL keep flams on steps that stay on. UNDO brings removed flams back.

The randomiser decides what each lane is from its name first, so renaming a lane changes how it's treated. If the name doesn't say, it goes by the lane's General MIDI note.

13Exporting MIDI

Click EXPORT MIDI for a menu:

  • Pattern N (playing) saves the playing pattern as a .mid file.
  • All patterns, in a row saves every non-empty slot one after another in a single file.
  • All patterns as separate files saves one file per non-empty slot into a folder you choose.
  • The chain, in a row saves the chain as one file, pattern after pattern (when a chain is set).

Drag the EXPORT MIDI key onto a MIDI track in your DAW to drop the playing pattern in as a clip.

Exported files use each lane's note and channel, the current VELOCITY, ACCENT, GATE, SWING, FLAM, LENGTH and the song tempo, with 16th-note steps. Flams, drags and ruffs are written as grace notes before the step.

14Saving and defaults

Everything is saved with your DAW project: all 12 patterns with their flams, the playing slot, the drum map (and whether it was edited), every knob and switch, and the MIDI port. If the saved port isn't connected when the project opens, the status line says so, and TRoss Seq reconnects to a port with the same name when it's available.

PRESETS → Save map + MIDI port as default stores your lane names, notes, channels, locks and port, and every new copy of TRoss Seq starts with them. Saved drum maps live in Documents/TRoss Seq/Maps and are shared by all your projects. The HELP setting is remembered for every copy of TRoss Seq.

Ableton Drum Rack: the 16 pads of a Drum Rack sit on C1 to D#2. The map follows the General MIDI layout most Ableton kits use. Pads E1 (40) and B1 (47) are left over: set a lane to them with NOTE or LEARN if your kit has a sound there. Each Ableton kit puts its own sounds on the pads, so rename lanes (Shift-click) to match yours.

AudioRealism ADM: put ADM in Note mode (its own sequencer off). Notes from the ADM manual's note-mode table, using the unaccented keys; TRoss Seq's accented steps (velocity above 100) play ADM's accents. Lane 13 triggers ADM's PCF (Pattern Controlled Fx); the randomiser leaves it alone.

16Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to check
The drum box plays nothingIs the song playing and RUN on? Does the MIDI OUT PORT status read CONNECTED? Do the lanes' CH match the channel the drum box listens on (often 10)? Are the notes right for your drum box (PRESETS, LEARN)? Is the lane muted, or is another lane soloed (S lit)? For a software plug-in, check the routing in section 3 for your DAW.
Every note plays twiceBoth outputs reach the same drum box. Turn HOST OUT off, or set MIDI OUT PORT to NONE.
One sound doesn't triggerThat lane's note isn't what the drum box expects. Click LEARN on the lane and hit the pad on the drum box (if it sends MIDI), or look up the note in its manual.
Port shows COULDN'T OPEN (Windows)Most Windows MIDI drivers let only one program use a port. Switch that port off in your DAW's MIDI settings, close other MIDI apps, then pick it again in TRoss Seq.
Port shows NOT CONNECTEDThe port saved in the project isn't plugged in. Connect it and pick it again, or choose another.
Drum box is early or late against the DAWAdjust PORT DELAY until they line up: turn it right if the drum box is early, left (negative) if it's late.
Drum box's tempo / effects don't followSwitch CLOCK OUT on and set the drum box to sync to external MIDI clock.
RANDOM doesn't change anything at onceWhile playing, the result waits for step 1; the display shows PAT 1 > RND. Check the lane isn't locked (padlock).
Keyboard doesn't play or recordArm the TRoss Seq track (in Live you can also set its monitor to In). MIDI THRU must be on to pass it to the drum box; REC must be on and the song playing to record.
The pop-up tips get in the waySwitch HELP off (top right). Knob values still show while you turn them.
A flam doesn't sound like a flamSome drum boxes cut a sound short when it retriggers quickly, or ignore low velocities. Try a longer FLAM time, a higher FLAM LVL, or raise VELOCITY.
TRoss Seq doesn't appear in the DAWRescan plug-ins (Live: Settings → Plug-Ins, Option-click Rescan on a Mac; Logic: Plug-in Manager; Bitwig: Settings → Locations, rescan). In Live check Audio Units / VST3 are switched on. On a Mac, check the installer actually ran (Privacy & Security → Open Anyway).
